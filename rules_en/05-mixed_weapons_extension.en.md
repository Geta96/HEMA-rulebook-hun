# Mixed Weapons
<span id="MIX"></span>

## General rules for Mixed Weapons
<span id="MIX-1"></span>

### Weapon categories and use
<span id="MIX-1.1"></span>

**MIX-1.1.1**
**One-handed weapons:** Any weapon with a one-handed grip is considered a one-handed weapon, regardless of blade length. The grip is 25 cm or shorter.

**MIX-1.1.2**
A fencer may hold a one-handed weapon by its grip or pommel, as they prefer.

**MIX-1.1.3**
**Two-handed weapons:** Any weapon with a two-handed grip is considered a two-handed weapon, regardless of blade length. The grip is longer than 25 cm.

**MIX-1.1.4**
Two-handed weapons must generally be held with both hands. If used with one hand, the hand must hold the grip directly below the crossguard or basket. With a two-handed weapon, scoring a hit while holding only the pommel/end of the grip is prohibited. Attempting to do so incurs a yellow-card penalty.

**MIX-1.1.5**
To change their grip, a fencer may release the weapon with their primary hand and hold it only with their rear hand, but fencing in this grip is prohibited.

**MIX-1.1.6**
A fencer may use the following weapon combinations at the same time:
- One one-handed weapon.
- Two one-handed weapons.
- Three one-handed weapons (in special cases, if they can be held safely; for example, a basket-hilted sword in the right hand, a shield strapped to the left forearm, and a dagger held in the left hand).
- One two-handed weapon.
- One two-handed weapon and one one-handed weapon (a special case, for example, using a longsword or hand-and-a-half sword with a shield, or a longsword with a dagger, provided the fencer can hold both safely). A fencer may change how they hold their weapons during a bout (for example, taking a longsword in one hand), as this does not count as a weapon change.

**MIX-1.1.7**
Dropping a weapon incurs a yellow-card penalty.

### Weapon combination rules
<span id="MIX-1.2"></span>

**MIX-1.2.1**
When registering for the competition, each fencer must declare their primary weapon style (primary weapon), which may not be changed during the competition. This may be a one- or two-handed weapon. Even weapons traditionally used as secondary weapons may be declared as a primary weapon (for example, a buckler, parrying dagger, or cloak).

**MIX-1.2.2**
Fencers must use their primary weapon in every bout of the competition.

**MIX-1.2.3**
In addition to their primary weapon, a fencer may have secondary weapons, which may be one- or two-handed. A fencer may change their secondary weapon between bouts, but not during the same bout.

**MIX-1.2.4**
During a bout, a fencer may put down or discard a secondary weapon if they prefer to continue fighting with fewer weapons, but may not pick up a new secondary weapon during the bout. Switching to a different weapon style or combination during a bout is prohibited.

**MIX-1.2.5**
If a weapon is damaged during a bout, the fencer may replace it with a weapon of the same style (for example, a rapier with a rapier, or a messer with a messer). If no replacement weapon is available, the fencer must continue with their remaining weapon(s).

**MIX-1.2.6**
If a fencer cannot replace their primary weapon, they may continue fighting with their secondary weapon, provided it can score points. If they have no weapon capable of scoring and cannot obtain a replacement of their original weapon style, the fencer loses all their points, may not continue the bout, and their opponent is declared the winner.

**MIX-1.2.7**
**Changing a weapon within the same weapon style:** The primary weapon style may not be changed during the competition (see MIX-1.2.1), and the secondary weapon may only be changed between bouts (see MIX-1.2.3). Two weapons are considered to be of the same style if their blade type, length, and handling are substantially the same; a difference in the grip or guard construction alone does not make them different weapon styles.

The following are examples of weapons of the same style (replacement is permitted):
- Swept-hilt rapier ↔ cup-hilt rapier.
- Straight one-handed sidesword with a knucklebow and side rings ↔ straight one-handed sidesword with simple finger rings—the only difference is the guard construction.

The following are examples of different weapon styles (replacement is not permitted):
- Katana ↔ sabre.
- Longsword ↔ okatana/nodachi.
- Knucklebow cavalry sabre ↔ bowl-hilt sabre.
- Sidesword ↔ rapier.

In borderline cases, the Head Referee may decide whether to allow the replacement based on the logic of the ruleset.

### Method of scoring a hit and valid target
<span id="MIX-1.3"></span>

**MIX-1.3.1**
With one-handed weapons, a valid hit may be scored with the point, edge, spine, or flat of the blade.

**MIX-1.3.2**
With two-handed weapons, a valid hit may be scored with the point, edge, spine, or flat of the blade, and with the pommel only. The pommel means only the purpose-made pommel at the end of the grip. If the weapon's construction does not include a pommel, a hit may be scored in the same way with the very end of the grip.

**MIX-1.3.3**
Contact with a weapon's fittings (crossguard, basket, knucklebow, parrying ring, or cup guard) is not a valid hit and scores no points. Such contact does not stop the bout; the action continues.

**MIX-1.3.4**
A hit to the weapon grip is valid and has the same value as a hit to the back of the hand.

**MIX-1.3.5**
Weapons without blades (for example, shields or cloaks) cannot score points; they may only be used for defence, distraction, and controlling the opponent's weapon.

**MIX-1.3.6**
Weapons may not be thrown. The only exception is a cloak, which may be thrown to distract the opponent. Throwing or dropping a weapon incurs a penalty card.

**MIX-1.3.7**
**One-handed weapons with short blades (blade length of 50 cm or less):** Unlike the rule in MIX-1.3.1, these weapons may only be used to thrust; striking and cutting are prohibited. As the basket and crossguard are very close to the blade's cutting edge, an attack with the edge would be dangerous. A valid hit may only be scored with the point; contact with the edge or flat does not score a point.

### Scoring and afterblow
<span id="MIX-1.4"></span>

**MIX-1.4.1**
The Afterblow system rules apply to every bout, regardless of whether a weapon is one- or two-handed, or whether it is held in one or both hands.

**MIX-1.4.2**
**Priority target — 2 points:** Head, torso, upper arm.
**Simple target — 1 point:** Forearm, hand, leg.

**MIX-1.4.3**
An afterblow deducts points from the value of the preceding hit only if the afterblow lands on a Priority target.

**MIX-1.4.4**
In the case of simultaneous hits, the fencer who hit a Priority target is awarded 1 point. If both fencers hit a Priority target, or neither hits a Priority target in a simultaneous exchange, neither fencer is awarded a point.

**MIX-1.4.5**
If a fencer hits with both weapons within a single fencing phase, the hit with the higher point value is valid.

**MIX-1.4.6**
If a fencer hits with both weapons, but more than one fencing phase elapses between the two hits, points may be awarded for the hit that landed first.

**MIX-1.4.7**
If a fencer hits simultaneously with both weapons while their opponent also hits with one weapon, points must be awarded according to which fencer hit a Priority target. In judging simultaneous actions, hitting with two weapons does not take precedence over hitting with one weapon.

## Equipment
<span id="MIX-2"></span>
The following are the rules for equipment (protective gear and weapons). Ultimately, all equipment is assessed individually, and equipment may be excluded from the competition if it is not considered safe in the context of the event, even if it otherwise complies with the wording of the rules.

### Hand and body protection
<span id="MIX-2.1"></span>

**MIX-2.1.1**
During a bout, fencers must wear protective equipment over their entire body, including their hands, that provides protection against the heaviest weapon used in the competition.

**MIX-2.1.2**
**Longsword Level Glove:**
A heavy glove with a hard shell made of rigid plastic or another dense, impact-resistant material, capable of absorbing blows from heavy two-handed weapons without bruising the fingers. It may be a mitten or five-finger design.
*Examples:* Sparring Glove Mittens/Special/Infinity, HF Armory Black Knight/Black Prince, Spes Lobster.

**MIX-2.1.3**
**Sabre Level Glove:**
A lighter, more mobile glove with built-in impact protection (hard plates, thick impact-resistant foam, or multiple layers of hardened leather) that relies on the sword's metal basket to provide actual hand protection. Hard fingertip protection is mandatory, as the sword grip does not protect that part of the hand.
*Examples:* Fenince Glove, HF Armory Firestone, HF Armory Black Knight Saber, Red Dragon.
These gloves may only be used if the weapon's hand guard protects the hand against most possible cutting angles.

**MIX-2.1.4**
**Soft Gloves:**
Leather or textile gloves (which may contain a puncture-resistant layer) that may offer some impact resistance but cannot prevent injury from strong, direct blows. Most weapons' hand guards do not provide enough coverage to make using these gloves safe. Their use is prohibited with most weapons.
They are permitted only in **exceptional** cases where the weapon completely covers the hand (for example, a broadsword with a basket surrounding the back of the hand from five directions, or a large shield where the hand is far from the edge and protected by a boss).
These gloves may not be used with complex-hilt rapiers or basket-hilt sabres.
These gloves do not provide adequate wrist protection, so they may only be used if the weapon (shield) or other protective equipment adequately protects the wrist.
Their use in a competition is permitted only in special cases with the prior approval of the chief organisers.
*Examples:* PBT rapier gloves, motorcycle gloves.

**MIX-2.1.5**
The wrist, back of the hand, knuckles, fingers, and fingertips must always be covered by hard protection (either the glove or the weapon's basket). A single knucklebow on the grip is not sufficient protection against cuts from every angle, except if the weapon has a cup guard and a short grip. A sabre basket or protection consisting of at least two knucklebows provides sufficient protection against cuts for using a Sabre Level Glove.

**MIX-2.1.6**
Weapons that do not meet the requirements for using a Sabre Level Glove must be used with a Longsword Level Glove.

**MIX-2.1.7**
During weapon registration, weapons that require a Longsword Level Glove or Sabre Level Glove must be marked at the tip with tape of a different colour.

### Weapon specifications
<span id="MIX-2.2"></span>

**MIX-2.2.1**
**Blade:**
- **Blade construction:** Straight and curved blades are both permitted. Weapons must be suitable and safe for freeplay. Blades must have thick, blunted edges. The blade tip must be rolled or spatulated.
- **Blade material:** Flexible carbon steel and heat-treated spring steel blades are permitted.

**MIX-2.2.2**
Modified weapons (for example, with parts sawn off or weights glued or taped on) are prohibited. Weapons with replaceable parts are not considered illegally modified. Weapons with repaired or replaced grips, including those wrapped in leather or cord, are also not considered illegally modified.

**MIX-2.2.3**
**Condition:** The blade must have no sharp or chipped areas, burrs, or rust, and there must be no cracks in the blade or any other part of the weapon.

**MIX-2.2.4**
**Safety of contact surfaces:** Every part of the weapon that could come into contact with the opponent—regardless of whether it is considered a valid target for scoring purposes (for example, the crossguard, basket, knucklebow, parrying ring, cup guard, or pommel)—must be blunt and free of burrs.

**MIX-2.2.5**
**Dimensions and weight:**
- **Maximum overall length:** 140 cm
- **Maximum blade length:** 101 cm (blade length only; excluding the ricasso on complex-hilt swords)
- **Maximum crossguard length:** 32 cm
- **Maximum diameter of parrying ring / complex basket / cup guard / sabre basket:** 16 cm
- **Point of balance (PoB):** 5–15 cm
- **Weight:** 700–1450 g

**MIX-2.2.6**
**Blade flexibility:** (Measured by applying pressure from the crossguard until the blade bends significantly.)
The maximum permitted bending force depends on blade length and the typical characteristics of weapons within that range. The thresholds depend only on blade length, not on the names of individual weapon types; examples in parentheses are for information only.
- **Two-handed sword:** Max. 15 kg
- **One-handed sword, short blade (up to 50 cm):** Max. 11 kg (for example, parrying daggers)
- **One-handed sword, medium blade (over 50 cm and up to 80 cm):** Max. 15 kg (for example, messers, arming swords, sideswords)
- **One-handed sword, long blade (over 80 cm):** Max. 11 kg (for example, sabres, rapiers)

Blades whose length is exactly at a category boundary must be classified in the shorter category.
(For example, a 80 cm blade falls into the short category, to which the 15 kg bending-force limit applies.)

### Parrying weapon specifications
<span id="MIX-2.3"></span>

**MIX-2.3.1**
**Shields:**
Shields may be held by a central grip or strapped to the arm. A shield has a handle and/or straps that can be fastened to the forearm.
Shields may be round, oval, elongated, or triangular. Pointed ends or protrusions are not permitted, regardless of material (metal, wood, hard plastic, hard rubber, etc.).
The shield's structure may be made of steel, wood, or hard plastic. It may optionally have a covering made of leather, textile, or foam.
- **Maximum diameter (largest dimension):** 60 cm
- **Maximum weight:** 3 kg
No points may be scored with a shield, and striking with a shield is prohibited.

**MIX-2.3.2**
**Pushing with a shield:** A sword or the opponent's hand may be pushed aside with a shield, but striking them with it is prohibited. Pushing the opponent's body with a shield is not permitted.

**MIX-2.3.3**
Pushing the opponent's hand aside with a sword is judged in the same way as grabbing it (see MIX-2.4.6 and MIX-2.4.7): the Head Referee stops the bout, and only a hit initiated almost immediately after the hand is pushed aside is considered valid.

**MIX-2.3.4**
**Two parrying weapons (for example, two shields, a shield and a cloak, or two cloaks):** Using two parrying weapons at the same time is permitted. Since parrying weapons (shields, cloaks) cannot score points, a fencer using two parrying weapons may score only by forcing their opponent, without violence, to cross the piste boundary completely (both feet outside the piste).
Pushing, shoving, or knocking the opponent off the piste using body contact is prohibited. Non-violent displacement off the piste is available to every fencer regardless of weapon category, so pushing an opponent with two parrying weapons does not give that fencer an advantage over the other. A fencer’s first step off the piste results in a warning; subsequent instances award a point to the opponent.

**MIX-2.3.5**
If one of the two parrying weapons can be attached to the forearm (for example, a shield strapped to the forearm), the fencer may also hold a one-handed weapon. For manageability, only a short or short-bladed one-handed weapon (blade length of 80 cm or less) may be used in this case.

**MIX-2.3.6**
**Cloak:**
- **Material:** Heavy, durable fabric capable of deflecting cuts (weights sewn into it or hard/sharp objects may not be attached).
- **Maximum length:** 150 cm
A cloak cannot score points; it may only be used for defence, distraction, and controlling the opponent's weapon.
A cloak may be thrown to distract and impede the opponent.

### Rules for the unarmed hand
<span id="MIX-2.4"></span>

**MIX-2.4.1**
When using an unarmed hand, a Longsword Level Glove must be worn on that hand, even if a more mobile glove (for example, a Sabre Level Glove) is permitted on the weapon hand because of the weapon's fittings.

**MIX-2.4.2**
It is prohibited to grab, strike, or parry a moving (cutting or thrusting) sword. A Priority target may not be covered with the unarmed hand.

**MIX-2.4.3**
If a fencer covers a Priority target with their unarmed hand, 2 points must be awarded against them. If they cover a Simple target, 1 point must be awarded against them.

**MIX-2.4.4**
If a fencer attempts to parry by putting their hand into a blade attack but does not cover a target zone, 1 point must be awarded against them.

**MIX-2.4.5**
A fencer may grip a stationary, bound (non-moving) weapon at the following locations:
- the grip;
- the basket, crossguard, parrying ring, or other fitting;
- the base of the blade, near the fittings;
- any surface of a parrying weapon (shield or cloak).

**MIX-2.4.6**
Wrestling is not permitted (Level 2 wrestlig action), but a fencer may momentarily grab the other fencer's arm to facilitate a hit and/or for defence (Level 1 wrestling action). If this is not followed by a hit almost immediately, the Head Referee must stop the bout. Twisting, jerking, or yanking the opponent's hand is prohibited. Only a single controlled push or pull, or impeding the arm by pinning it, is permitted.

**MIX-2.4.7**
In the case of an arm grab, the Referee stops the bout immediately. A valid hitting action must begin before the Referee calls "Halt!" (after the Referee notices the action but before issuing the command), and no later than the moment the arm or weapon is controlled.
